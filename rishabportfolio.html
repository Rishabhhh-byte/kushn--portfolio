Executive Summary

KUSHN manufactures personalized replacement ear cushions for over-ear headphones and headsets, using smartphone-based ear scanning, a proprietary geometry-to-CAD pipeline, and 3D printing. The core insight: headphone discomfort isn't a foam problem, it's a geometry problem — and no one in the market is solving it at the anatomical level.

The elevator pitch

Millions of people wear headphones and headsets for four or more hours a day, and current designs don't fit their ears. The industry has spent decades improving materials — softer foam, cooling gel, memory pads — while leaving the actual mismatch between generic pad geometry and individual ear anatomy untouched. KUSHN closes that gap: scan your ear with a phone, convert it to a manufacturable 3D model, and 3D-print a cushion built for your anatomy, compatible across major headphone and headset brands.

02

The Problem

Millions rely on headphones and headsets for work, learning, and entertainment — but current designs don't fit their ears.

Pressure points

Localized pressure on ear cartilage causes pain and long-term discomfort.

Heat buildup

Trapped heat and sweat lead to ear fatigue, itchiness, and reduced focus.

Discomfort, early

Most users feel discomfort within just 2–3 hours of continuous use.

The Interface Paradox

The real problem isn't foam or material quality — it's the geometric mismatch between generic, one-size-fits-all pad shapes and individual ear anatomy. Better foam treats a symptom. Better geometry treats the cause. Pressure-mapping studies and user interviews with gamers and remote workers confirm the discomfort is geometry-driven, not material-driven — but there's an honest gap here too: we don't yet have published willingness-to-pay data, and many consumers replace an entire headphone rather than a worn-out pad.

Our Solution

Personalized ear cushions, designed for you — from ear scan to a perfectly fitted cushion, in three steps.

Step 1

Scan

An ear scan captured using the user's own smartphone via photogrammetry. Quick, accurate, and needs no special hardware.

Step 2

Design

A proprietary geometry engine converts the scan into a precise 3D model, optimized for comfort, fit, and multi-brand compatibility.

Step 3

Manufacture

3D printing and finishing produce a cushion custom-manufactured for that individual's anatomy, using durable, high-quality materials.

The wider competitive field

Beyond direct replacement-pad players, several adjacent threats matter: Bose Fit Kit (depth-sensing ear measurement), Sennheiser Pro and Plantronics/Poly (enterprise headset ecosystems with comfort features already built in), Materialise and other general 3D-manufacturing services that could pivot into ear cushions, and budget players like Anker Soundcore who iterate quickly on cheap memory foam.

Critical vulnerability to plan around: Wavelet, Phonak, and Widex already run ear-scanning customization at scale in the billion-dollar hearing-aid market. If any major headphone OEM licenses that approach or acquires a player like Wavelet, the core KUSHN workflow becomes commoditized quickly. Our response to this is addressed directly in Section 09.

Where the gap actually is

Call centers

No vendor has solved headset discomfort as an occupational-health issue. ~800K–13.5M addressable agents; a 15–20% productivity gain implies $15K–30K in savings per 100-agent team, per year.

Aviation / professional audio

Pressure points on extended-wear headsets matter for pilots on 8+ hour flights. ~150K users, $200–400 ASP, with an FAA/EASA compliance angle that acts as a soft regulatory moat.

Overall assessment: weak, and we're saying so. Our edge today is first-mover speed and manufacturing execution, not a structural moat. A well-capitalized competitor could replicate the workflow in 12–18 months once the model is proven. The only durable moat available to us is data and clinical validation — 18–24 months of customer comfort and productivity data, ideally with third-party validation, that turns "we sell cushions" into "we're the only ones with the proof."

Top risks & mitigations

1

Market treats this as "nice-to-have," not "must-have"

Critical impact35–40% probability

D2C customers may not prioritize comfort enough to pay $80–120 when $20 alternatives exist.

Mitigation: Pivot weight to B2B occupational health, where ROI is measurable and the buyer isn't the end user. De-prioritize D2C until enterprise product-market fit is proven.
2

3D-printing COGS can't get below ~$20/unit at volume

Critical impact45–50% probability

Unit economics tighten if per-unit manufacturing cost doesn't fall with scale.

Mitigation: Lock manufacturing-partner pricing at 100/250/500/1,000-unit tiers up front; explore injection-molded variants for high-volume SKUs.
3

An OEM (Sony, Bose, Sennheiser) builds or acquires a competing solution

Critical impact50–60% probability

Wavelet, Phonak, and Widex already do this in hearing aids; the crossover risk is real.

Mitigation: Move fast on enterprise lock-in before the threat crystallizes; treat IP licensing to an OEM as a credible, planned exit path rather than a failure mode.
4

Smartphone ear-scan quality is inconsistent for a meaningful share of users

High impact30–40% probability

Fails to produce a usable scan for an estimated 20%+ of users without a fallback.

Mitigation: Test scanning across 500+ ear shapes before scaling; build an in-person scanning fallback and improve ML robustness in the app.
5

Enterprise sales cycles run 6–9 months, burning cash before revenue lands

Medium impact40–50% probability

Each pilot costs $5K–10K before any contract revenue arrives.

Mitigation: Secure enough pre-seed runway (12–18 months) up front; focus on 3–5 beachhead customers rather than spreading across 20 simultaneous pilots.

Execution Roadmap

A specific, founder-executable 18-month plan — not theory.

Weeks 1–4 · MVP

Prototype, validation planning & manufacturing

  • Scan 50+ ear shapes across the IIMB community to test app accuracy
  • Get manufacturing quotes from three 3D-printing partners for 100 units
  • Map 10+ call centers across Bangalore, Pune, and Hyderabad with named decision-makers
  • Open a biomechanics lab partnership for independent pressure-mapping validation
  • Lock CAD design logic and order the first 100-unit production run
Target: 80%+ clean scans, <10% manufacturing defects
Weeks 5–12 · Validation

Call-center outreach and pilot programs

  • Run a structured outreach sequence (email → phone) to book pilot conversations
  • Commit to three 30-day pilots (10–15 agents each), free, as research partnerships
  • Run a weekly comfort survey plus employer-provided quality and sick-day metrics
  • Document pilot results as one-pagers and a public case study
Target: 3 pilots running, 60%+ average comfort improvement
Months 4–6 · Revenue

Convert pilots to paid production contracts

  • Convert at least one of three pilots into a paid production contract
  • Expand outreach into Tier-2 city call centers with less competition
  • Explore white-label revenue-share partnerships with call-center consultancies
Target: 1–2 paid customers, first meaningful monthly revenue
Months 5–8 · Fundraising

Pre-seed fundraising sprint

  • Build a 10-slide pitch deck around Problem → Solution → Traction → Market → Model → Team → Ask
  • Pursue angel investors, pre-seed VCs, and the Startup India Seed Fund in parallel
Target: pre-seed round committed
Months 9–18 · Scale

Product iteration and enterprise expansion

  • Build a lightweight web dashboard for agent onboarding and HR admin visibility
  • Lock a second, larger production run (250–500 units) with the manufacturing partner
  • Hire the first non-founder team member, focused on enterprise sales
Target: 5–10 enterprise customers, 500+ agents covered

The pivot we're building toward

Our core IP — the ear-scan-to-CAD pipeline plus manufacturing — is worth more repositioned as an occupational-health platform than as a standalone headphone accessory. KUSHN Wellness combines custom ear pads, a wear-time and fatigue sensor, a productivity dashboard, and an ergonomic recommendations engine, sold to call-center operators and HR platforms as a compliance and wellness tool priced at $200–300 per agent per year. That reframes the addressable market from roughly $500M in headphone accessories to a nascent $5–10B call-center wellness SaaS category, with stickier, recurring revenue and a much clearer path to an enterprise-grade acquisition.
